# Sea and weather

Read before working on wind, rain, waves, storms, currents or the sea map. The code is in `RoughSeas/SeaAndWeather/`; what draws the sea is in `RoughSeas/Storms/`.

## The shape

The server owns the sea and weather; a client reads a map of it and computes nothing.

```
server                                         client
SeaModel (ISeaModel, ISeaSource)               SeaMapClient (ISeaSource)
├─ SeaWeatherModel: storms, background wind,   ├─ snapshots from the region's moddata
│  showers, currents                           │  and from the roughseas:seamap channel
├─ OceanBasins: the ocean map + calibration    └─ interpolated in space and in time
├─ WaterBody: fetch and depth upwind
└─ WindSea: the waves those raise                SurfaceClock → SurfaceAim → SurfaceField + SeaSurface
SeaMapServer ── snapshot per region ─────────→  the water shader, vessels, floating items
└─ Sent: the map as clients hold it
   └─ SwellAround → SurfaceAim: the drawn sea around a player in the water
```

`SeaWeatherSystem` (`ISeaWeather`) is the door on both sides: `At` and `CurrentAt` answer from `Source`, which is the model on the server and the received map on a client. `Model` is the server's alone and throws on a client. `Map` is the map clients draw the sea from on either side: the received one on a client, the copy of what it sent on the server.

## The map

- `SeaMapServer` ticks every 250 ms. It refreshes the map regions within one of each player's, each every `map.updateSeconds`, and stops for the tick once `map.tickBudgetMilliseconds` are spent.
- A snapshot (`SeaMapSnapshot`) holds a grid of `map.cellBlocks` cells: wind, storm presence, rain, wave height and period, current, water temperature, the water's depth. Each field is an Int16 at its own scale, with explicit protobuf tags and a version.
- It goes into the map region's moddata (`roughseas:seamap`), so a client that loads the region has it at once, and over the channel to the players near it.
- `SeaMapClient` blends a cell with its neighbours bilinearly, and from the last snapshot to the new one over the time between them, by the server's stamps, so clients holding the same two snapshots show the same values at the same world time. A region older than `map.staleHours` counts as no data.
- A snapshot also carries the region's wave generations for the slot before its time, its own and the next (`SeaModel.GenerationOf`): the direction and period of the highest sea among the region and its eight neighbours when each `seaSampleHours` slot began. They are cached per region and slot; the first of a place costs what the snapshot's own cells cost on cold caches.

## What the model stands on

- **The ocean map is the world's own.** A built region's `OceanMap`; where nothing is built yet, `GenMaps.oceanGen` read through `GeneratedOcean` (small tiles, cached). That layer is not thread-safe, so `OceanGenLock` locks its every call, vanilla's chunk thread included. Never build a second layer.
- **The map's numbers mean what the worldgen makes them mean.** `OceanCalibration` learns from the built world where sea starts on it: salt water at sea level, column by column, only in loaded chunks, each column once, by balanced error. It settles at `ocean.calibrationSamples`, is saved with the world (`roughseas:oceancalibration`, versioned), and the model forgets what it brewed from the default.
- **Climate is vanilla's.** A storm's birth comes from seed, worldgen climate, latitude, season and time. Its strength follows the season's north-south temperature gradient, which `SeaWeatherSystem.SeasonalCelsius` takes from the game's own climate chain (`TriggerOnGetClimate`, as `GetClimateAt` runs it), so a mod that moves temperatures moves the storms. Its expression (rain, snow, thunder) reads the live temperature.
- **Storms follow measurements, per day and area.** Each type is born at `birthsPerMillionKm2PerDay`, turned into a chance per cell and slot through the horizontal scale; a day is a real day and only the calendar is the game's. Every storm number in `seaweather.json` carries its source; the wiki's *The science behind it* gathers them. `/bridge climate` checks the result over years of storms at a grid of points.

## Waves

- Young & Verhagen (1996), as CEM II-2 gives it, for every depth, with the depth's period cap Tp ≤ 9.78√(d/g).
- Fetch is the straight line upwind: over the built world in `waves.fetchStepBlocks` steps while it is built, over the ocean map in `waves.openSeaStepBlocks` beyond, up to `waves.maxFetchBlocks`. It is scaled by the world's horizontal compression, `waves.realPolarEquatorMetres` over the world's `polarEquatorDistance` (×200 by default). Depth is not scaled.
- Depth is worldgen's seabed where water still reaches it, and sounded block by block where the world changed. Beyond the built world it is the built depth measured so far.
- Duration: the wind's mean over each of `seaBuildHours` becomes an equivalent fetch (CEM II-2-38), and the highest sea of those windows stands. The means are weighted by time over samples every `seaSampleHours`, so a brief storm long ago stays brief. A squall raises a young short sea; a storm builds one over hours.
- Above `waves.kneeMetres` a soft knee bends the height toward `waves.ceilingMetres`.

## How it meets vanilla

- **Wind.** `VanillaRegions` (server, every 2 s, while vanilla's `autoChangePatterns` is on) has `RegionWinds` lease each region our Beaufort pattern (`config/windpatterns/beaufort.json`, `roughseas-force0`–`12`) at the region's exact strength. A pattern someone else set keeps the region until it ends. `VanillaWind` runs after vanilla's `OnGetWindSpeed` and turns vanilla's answer to the map's direction and finer detail.
- **Rain.** `VanillaRain` writes into `OnGetClimate` after vanilla's handler, and steps aside for `OverridePrecipitation`.
- **Events.** Vanilla's roll runs. Our transpiler makes it read current rain and pick by each event's own conditions. `StormEvents` pins a storm type's event where a storm covers a region and its conditions hold.
- **Order.** `SeaWeatherSystem.ExecuteOrder` is 1: after vanilla's weather (0.1), before the Devastation's effects (2).
- **Patch priorities.** A postfix that sets a value runs first (`Priority.High`), so other mods' postfixes adjust ours. Patches that only read or adjust run late (`Priority.Low`), the transpiler too. The ocean layer's lock is outermost.
- **Units.** Vanilla's wind 1.0 is 25 m/s (`vanilla.unitsPerMps`), checked against its `storm` and `mediumbreeze` patterns.

## One system of water

Everything about water, from the deep sea to the shore's sheet, is one pipeline in `RoughSeas/Water/`. Data runs one way, each stage owns its quantities, and the next reads them through its interface. A new mechanic reads a stage; it never works one out again.

```
WaveTheory, WaterColumn   the formulas; read by all
SeaModel (climate) → WaveTrains ─┐
SeabedMap (the bed) ─────────────┴→ WaveTransformation → SurfField → SeaSurface → foam (seafoam.fsh)
                                        └→ SwashSheets ──────┘           └→ WaveHere: hulls, sound, strikes
```

- `WaveTheory`: gravity, dispersion, group speed, Iribarren, the sea's heights, the bore. The shader takes its constants from here (`SEA_GRAVITY`, `SEA_BORE_AMPLITUDE`, `SEA_TWO_PERCENT`).
- `WaveTrains`: which waves run, their banks, handover, short waves and clock.
- `WaveTransformation`: what a bed does to waves: lag, gain, breaking share and the depth that sets their speed.
- `SwashSheets`, `Band`, `SheetFront`, `RunUp`: the same waves over land. `Groundwater` asks the same `Band` with the open sea's height, the upper bound of any sheet, so the water under the shore is always wider than what is drawn.
- `SurfField`: the waves over the bed around a player. Only it knows its texture layout; the rest calls it by name.
- `SeaSurface`: the water at a point and a moment. `seasurface.vsh` is its other language: a change to one is made to the other.
- `IDrawnSea.WaveHere` is the single question for the waves at a point. Ask it for height, period, direction, depth, slope, breaking share and the arriving sea. Don't judge breaking by height and depth, don't sound the depth for a wave, and don't take a wave's direction from the wind.

## Drawing it

- **The drawn sea is a function of the shared input and the world's clock**, never of a client's own history: two clients in one region draw the same crests. Nothing in it may ease from a client's last value or count its frames.
- `WaveTime` is the waves' clock: `SeaClock.Seconds`, the calendar's game seconds over vanilla's 60 × `CalendarSpeedMul` (its default 0.5 while the calendar is stopped), so it runs, stops and races as vanilla's foliage counters do (`DefaultShaderUniforms.Update`). It only smooths the calendar's whole-millisecond ticks and the server's corrections.
- `SurfaceClock` aims every `surface.aimSeconds`: `SurfaceField` samples the waves of the player's region and its neighbours into a 48 × 48 texture, `seaHeightMap`, each cell a share of the highest; `SeaSurface` takes that height and the foam of the highest cell's Beaufort force, and raises the trains of the region's generations.
- `SeaSurface` quantises a generation to a heading (`surface.turnDegrees`) and a length step (`surface.stretch`), so a slot that brings the same sea brings the same trains. New trains take over in the other bank over `surface.settleSeconds` from their slot's start, the same moment on every client. Entering another region hands over from what is drawn, keeping the stronger bank's weight and the total energy continuous.
- The shader, vessels and floating items read the same share (`SurfaceField.Factor`), so a boat rides the wave drawn under it.
- **What is in the sea is judged by the drawn surface, not by the water block.** `DrawnWater` (`StormsModSystem.DrawnWater`, on a client) gives the surface over a point's water column: the still water's surface where vanilla draws it (`LiquidLevel` eighths of the top water block, 7/8 for still water) plus `IDrawnSea.HeightAt` there, looked for below the point only as far as a crest there reaches, and none where a solid block stands between (a deck, a dock, the shore). The camera's underwater view and sound follow it (`EyeUnderSurface`, after vanilla's `GetEyesInWaterColorShift` and `EyesInWaterDepth`); a body in the water asks it too.
- **A hull is carried by the water the drawn waves move.** `IDrawnSea.HullVelocityAt` averages each train's linear orbital velocity over the hull's underwater body: its length along its heading, its beam, its draft, at the height it heaves to (`SeaSurface.HullVelocityAt`). A hull displaces its own mass, so it takes that velocity whatever it weighs, and its mass tells through its draft and its heave. `WaveRide` holds a vessel's one size (her length from `IHull.Shape`, her beam as `EntityBehaviorSeaForces.BeamMetres` gives it, across the outrigger's float) and one heave (`HullRider`), which `VesselMotion` draws and the water is read at. `WaveCarry` adds the velocity after `EntityBoat.updateBoatAngleAndMotion` on the side that moves her: the client that steers her, through the sea it draws, or the server while nobody does, through the region's `SwellAround` (`RegionSwells`, one a region, measured in `/storms budget`). The waves' velocity is per second of the waves' clock, so it is scaled to the tick's by `SeaClock.SecondsIn`. The waves' drift is whatever this gives; `CurrentDrift` carries the current alone, and the wind moves no unsteered hull until #184.
- **The server builds the drawn sea itself where a decision needs it.** The drawn sea is a function of the sea map, the wave generations and the world's clock, so the server keeps the map as its clients hold it (`SeaMapServer.Sent`, the snapshots it sent, read as `SeaWeatherSystem.Map` on either side), and `SurfaceAim` aims a surface the same way on both sides. `SwellAround` is that surface on the server: around a swimmer, run as long as the player is in or on the water and dropped when they leave it, and one a region for the vessels nobody steers (`RegionSwells`). It may differ from the client's for a few seconds in two cases: a handover between regions, and a region the client takes for the first time, which it reads at once while `Sent` still eases it. Breath is the first to read it: `BreathUnderSurface` postfixes vanilla's `EntityBehaviorBreathe.Check` and judges the eye against `DrawnWater` there. A crest takes the air; a trough gives it back only to an eye whose block is water. Riders keep vanilla's check, since their client lifts them onto the swell while the server holds the unlifted position. No world setting turns it off: drowning is vanilla's, matched here to the drawn surface.
- **The water is divided near the player, on the GPU.** `SurfaceTessellation` turns vanilla's `chunkliquid` and `chunkliquiddepth` programs into vertex, tessellation control and evaluation stages: a prefix on `CompileShader` compiles the program's own vertex shader, every text patch already in it, as the evaluation stage, and `CreateShaderProgram` attaches both; the liquid pools draw `Patches`. Each triangle edge's division comes from its midpoint's distance, so neighbours agree and no seam opens: doubling from a block at `surface.tessellation.reachMetres` to 1/`maxLevel` near the player. Vanilla's tile, light, tint, oceanity, flow, carriers, lakes and rivers come with the mesh, since it is still vanilla's. Below OpenGL 4.0 the water keeps one quad a block. `.surface tessellation false` compares; `bin/game water` reports the two passes' GPU time.
- **Short waves are ours; vanilla's ripple is not drawn over our sea.** Vanilla's noise (`vertexwarp.vsh`, about 0.86 m across, up to a quarter metre over ocean, knowing nothing of our waves) is kept by `1 - share`, so it stays only where our sea doesn't reach. `surface.shortWaves` lays octaves of the Phillips range between 8 and 0.5 m under the long trains, in `seaShort`: heights from the spectrum's variance, sines with no sway, in both banks, faded by the local vertex spacing so each density level drops what it can't carry (`smoothstep(2s, 4s, L)`, `seaMesh`). `DrawnWater` and the breath see them; `PoseOf` doesn't (`shortWaves: false`), since a hull averages out what is shorter than itself.
- **Vanilla's samplers stay on vanilla's units, in every program.** The engine numbers a program's samplers by a regex over its source, vertex shader first (`ShaderProgram.collectUniformNames`). `Use()` binds `liquidDepth`, `sky` and the shadow maps by that number; the generated setters, the cloud pass (`liquidDepth` on 4) and mods like SheyderMod (specular on 7) assume vanilla's. A sampler we declare in a shared include moves every one of them, and one we bind inside the range overwrites someone's texture: 2.0.0's `seaHeightMap` drew leaves black beside SheyderMod and wiped clouds over the sea.
- So `seaHeightMap` is declared `uniform highp sampler2D`, which the regex doesn't match; `SurfaceShader` looks up its location itself after each compile and binds it on the GPU's last texture unit (`GL_MAX_COMBINED_TEXTURE_IMAGE_UNITS - 1`), which no one hard-codes. A new sampler of ours follows the same rule: the seabed's eight (`SurfField.Samplers`) take the eight units below it. `.surface samplers` shows each program's numbering and where the height map is read.

## The shore

- **The water under the shore.** `Shore/Groundwater` puts the shore's own still water (salt or fresh, vanilla's block) into the fluid layer under the full blocks of the shore at its still-water level, rock included, as far as the swash reaches: `Band` throws a sheet from every cell of water beside land, by the run-up of the higher of the everyday sea (`SeaModel.EverydayWavesIn`: the background wind's mean, blowing the prevailing way, fully built) and the sea map's now, and follows it block by block (`SheetFront`). The band's reach is worked out again rather than recorded: one flood a water level, off the main thread, after a chunk loads, a block changes in or beside the band, or its shore's sea changes; each chunk is then wetted or drained to match at the water's spread delay. The water itself is vanilla's and is saved with the chunks: a world opened without the mod keeps it under its shores, hidden, and vanilla's readers see it as water there. Vanilla leaves a carrier alone (a full block is a barrier both ways); digging its block frees it as ordinary water, and the hole fills. `/shore here` reads the ground under the player and how far the nearest shore runs up; `[shore]` logs every chunk's band.
- **Open water.** A position holding a full solid block is land, whatever its fluid layer holds: every reader asks `OpenWater`, never the fluid layer alone. `VanillaShore` keeps vanilla's readers on land the same way: a random tick there goes to the solid block, liquid containers neither pick nor fill from it, `SearchFluidBlocks` (farmland) and the bobber's pond count leave it out.
- **The seabed.** `SurfMaker` reads the seabed around the player off the main thread (`SeabedMap`, a texel a block, `surface.shore` in `storms.json`) and builds a `SurfField`: the depth, the slope, each wave slot's lag over the shoaling bed (`PhaseField`, the eikonal solved by fast sweeping, so the crests turn by Snell's law), each slot's gain and the share of waves breaking (`WaveEnergy`), and over land the swash's distance from the shoreline, the shore's run-up and the run-up a wave needs to get there. The depth is sounded block by block down to `surface.shore.deepestBlocks`, so a bar a player built counts; below that it is worldgen's, and where worldgen knows no bed under the water it is open sea. `WaveEnergy` carries each train's energy along its turned crests over the bed, div(E c_g n) = -D, by upwind sweeps from deep water at the map's edge: shoaling and refraction are what that balance does, D is Battjes and Janssen's breaking, and a wave that breaks over a bar goes on smaller past it. The swell's height is never a function of the depth at a point. A wave's lag starts where it enters the map, at the edge or off land upwind, from `CoarseBed`: the same `WaveTransformation` over the three regions around, on the sea map's depths and heights, a cell of 32 blocks at a time, one reference for everyone reading the place. It hands the map the waves' energy as well (`EnteringWaves`): what `WaveEnergy` carries in at the map's edge, and the sea arriving where the breaking water runs off the map, so a reef beyond the map shelters the water behind it. Its land takes the nearest water's, so the reference has no holes beside a shore. `WaveEnergy` averages each train's direction over its own quarter wavelength, so rays don't cross into a caustic behind an islet. A broken wave runs over the water and its own height (`WaveTransformation`'s celerity depth). `seasurface.vsh` multiplies each slot by its gain, gives a broken wave a bore's shape and steepens a plunging breaker to the cusp (Iribarren); the breaking foam sits on the dominant train's front alone, so its edge doesn't run along the crest, and offshore foam gives way by the breaking share. Over land the shader draws the Shen & Meyer sheet over the water under the shore, each wave's run-up from its own height at the shoreline; the sheet is the CPU's only in `FaceStrikes` and `SheetFront`, so `HeightAt` reads no water over land. `seawet.fsh` darkens the ground the swash wets while it drains. `SwellAround` reads its own seabed on the server. `.surface shore off` draws the sea as if deep everywhere; `.surface bed N` reads the field under the player and `.surface probe X Z` why the sea stands as it does at a point: its share, height, crests, depth, gains; `.surface reflection|lag|energy false` leaves one term out, and `.surface colours true` paints the water by height with each block's outline, and `.surface map height|phase|breaking|foam` paints it as that quantity, the crests white lines on the phase view. A wave answers to the bed at its own scale: the depth behind its speed, its lag and the slope are averaged over a quarter wavelength to either side (`faces.smoothingWavelengths`, at most `faces.smoothingMaxBlocks`), while what breaks it is the depth under it, so a narrow bar stops it. Over the map's last `surface.shore.edgeFadeBlocks` blocks the waves go over to deep water's, since nothing is known of the bed beyond: the two wave fields are added by their shares (`SurfField.Faded`), not their phases mixed, because a lag of tens of radians mixed down to none over that width would squeeze the crests. Near a face, the reflection's distance and normal are blended over half the distance to the wall, so a change of nearest wall from cell to cell draws no crease.
- **Faces.** `FaceBuilder` reads every side of a full block a water column meets (`SeabedMap.CrownAt`, the ground's own read, a barrier's by its own walk) into the field's `Face` layer (`seaFace`): the distance to the nearest face through the water, its outward normal averaged over a quarter wavelength to either side, among faces not looking the opposite way, so a stair reflects as the wall it makes up and a thin wall keeps both its sides, and the reflection (`WallReflection`, Zanuttigh & van der Meer); the normal is written half length where the crest stands higher than the face, which then doesn't foam. The distance is measured from that same smoothed line, and the reflected lift grows from the face no steeper than `faces.reflectedSlopeLimit`, since a face's own vertices are shared with the water held under the land. `seasurface.vsh` and `SeaSurface` add the mirrored train; `HullVelocityAt` leaves it out. `Storms/FaceStrikes` decides each strike from the shared sea and the clock every `faces.tickMilliseconds` (the dominant slot's crest reaching the face, as `seaSwash` times a sheet), and `SprayParticles` spends the `Impact` that `WallSpray` computes on vanilla's async particles; the sheet stopped at a step comes from the band's own spread (`StepFace`, `SurfBuilder`). A step's foot is written as a face distance of -1.5 for the shader. Keep `seaFoamDecay` out of `seaFoam`: a vertex stage that used `seaFoam` makes other programs active for its uniforms.

## Rules that keep it whole

- Every decision logs a debug line: `[seas]` for the model, the map and vanilla's regions, `[storms]` for the sea's effects, `[shore]` for the water under the shore.
- Every number is in JSON: `seaweather.json`, `storms.json`, `shore.json`, `windpatterns/beaufort.json`.
- What we save is versioned. A world opens without the mod: its regions fall back to vanilla's patterns, and vanilla re-rolls them.
- Read a block's water through `OpenWater`, never the fluid layer alone, and its chunk only once `GetChunkAtBlockPos` finds it: an unloaded chunk reads as air. So does a chunk idle for 8 s through the lock-free accessor, which doesn't unpack it: off the main thread, `Unpack_ReadOnly` what you read first, as `SeabedMap.Read` does.
- Write the water under the shore through the world's own accessor, never the bulk one: its commit takes a fluid removed under a solid for the solid removed, and lowers the rain heightmap there.
