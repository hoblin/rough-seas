A damaged hull is mended with the stuff it was built from. Hold the material, right-click the hull, and one patch goes in. Each patch is hard work and makes you hungry, so a long mend at sea needs food as well as material.

| Vessel | Patch with | Each patch restores | Satiety per patch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Raft](Raft) | 1 log of its wood | a sixth | 60 |
| [Raft](Raft), bamboo | 3 bamboo stakes | a third | 60 |
| [Sailboat](Sailboat) | 10 planks of its wood | a fifteenth | 80 |
| [Dugout](Dugout) | 1 log of its wood | two fifths | 80 |
| [Coracle](Coracle) | 1 leather | a half | 40 |
| [Birch-bark canoe](Birch-Bark-Canoe) | 2 birch bark | a third | 40 |
| [Hide currach](Hide-Currach) | 1 rendered fat | a quarter | 40 |
| [Outrigger canoe](Outrigger-Canoe) | 2 planks of its wood | a fifth | 80 |
| [Reed boat](Reed-Boat) | cannot be mended | | |

- The wood must be the hull's own: an oak sailboat takes oak planks.
- A full mend costs about half of what the vessel took to build. A sailboat built from about 300 planks takes some 150 to bring back from nothing.
- A hull at full condition takes no more.
- Birch bark comes off a birch log with a knife.

The [hide currach](Hide-Currach) needs its fat whether it is struck or not: it [wears](Hull-Condition#wear) a tenth a day afloat, and one patch covers about two and a half days. The [reed boat](Reed-Boat) can't be mended at all: build another.

Mend in calm water. A patch can't keep up with a [breaking sea](Surf-and-Swamping), and a hull that [breaks](Hull-Condition#when-it-breaks) can't be mended back.
