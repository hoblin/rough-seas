In the water your body floats by what it displaces, not by a rule of the game. It rises, lies and heaves by the weight of the water it pushes aside, under the waves you see.

## Floating

Left alone in the sea you float with the water at your face, and the waves carry you up, down and to and fro. In a calm sea you can just breathe. In a rough one the crests wash over your head and the troughs give your air back. A [current](Currents) carries you with it, as it carries a boat.

Fresh water holds you up less, so in a lake or a river you sink slowly unless you swim. Holding jump strokes you up, so you tread water with your head clear.

## Wading

Until the water reaches your chest you walk and jump as in the base game, so a jump takes you up a one-block step out of the water. The water drags your way forward only by the part of you it covers: a jump with the water at your knees carries you on, and the deeper the water, the more of a jump's way it takes. Waves and a current push you as you wade.

## Swimming

Hold a direction to swim: each stroke pushes you through the water that way from where you look, with the power a trained swimmer swims with. You push hardest from a standstill, so you are under way within a second or two. In still water you swim about 0.9 m/s and glide on for a few seconds after you let go. The water you swim in moves you too: swim with a wave's front face and it carries you faster than you swim; swim against a current faster than you, and you lose ground.

## What you carry

Armour and the tools and weapons in your hotbar weigh on you, in the water and in a boat. Your backpack and materials don't: a stack of stones in your hand stays the game's convention. Move your tools and weapons into your backpack before a long swim.

You have about a kilogram and a half to spare in the sea. A knife only sinks you a little lower, an axe takes you under unless you tread water, and full plate takes you down fast. Extra weight doesn't drown you by itself: it makes you swim to stay up.

## Going deep

Once your chest is about 6 m down, the water squeezes the air in your lungs so far that you sink with nothing on you, and you keep sinking until you swim up. Above that depth you rise on your own, slowly.

## Breaking crests

A crest can break over you, as one breaks on a vessel: as often, by the same [wind](Wind-and-Waves). Its water is full of air and holds you up less, so you go under, and its roller pushes you along with the wave. The air clears within a wave or so and you come back up. The next crest can break before you do.

In [surf](Surf-and-Swamping#breakers) the tallest waves break, and close to the beach nearly all of them.

Holding jump doesn't keep you up through a breaker: its frothy water holds you up less, and your stroke can't make up the difference. A small one takes you a hand's breadth under, a big one a metre or more. You come back up once its air clears.

The world setting [Breakers on swimmers](World-Settings#the-mechanics) turns breaking crests off. You still float on the waves and breathe by them. The formulas are in [The science of a body in the water](The-Science-of-a-Body-in-the-Water#a-body-in-the-water).
