In the water your body floats by what it displaces, not by a rule of the game, and you swim with a trained swimmer's strength. You rise, lie and heave by the weight of the water you push aside, under the waves you see, and what you carry weighs on you by what it is made of.

## What is different from the base game

The base game holds a swimmer up by a rule and lets you swim fast whatever you carry. Here:

- You float with the water at your eyes in the sea, and lower in fresh water, where you sink slowly unless you swim.
- You swim at a trained swimmer's pace, about 1.2 m/s, not the base game's.
- Your armour and the tools in your hotbar weigh you down; wood holds you up.
- Waves lift you, currents carry you and breaking crests push you under.
- On the bottom you stand on your feet and push off it with your legs.

Each world can turn this off: with [Realistic swimming](World-Settings#the-mechanics) off you swim as in the base game, and [Swimming strength](World-Settings#swimming-strength) makes your stroke two or three times as strong.

## Floating

Left alone in the sea you float with the water at your eyes, and the waves carry you up, down and to and fro. Even in a calm sea your face is awash, and every crest covers it: hold jump to lift your head clear and breathe. A [current](Currents) carries you with it, as it carries a boat.

Fresh water holds you up less, so in a lake or a river you float lower and sink slowly. Holding jump strokes you up, so you tread water with your head clear.

## Jumping in

Jump in from a pier or a bank and you keep your way into the water. You go in feet first, so the water slows you down along your length and you plunge deep: from a metre up, past 5 m. Your way forward goes much sooner, since the water takes you broadside. Below about 6 m you sink, so hold jump or swim up to come back to the surface. Holding a direction as you go in, you start to swim once the water has slowed you to your swimming pace.

## Wading

Until the water reaches your chest your legs and the base game's jump move you, so a jump takes you up a one-block step out of the water. The water drags you only by the part of you it covers: with it at your knees a jump carries you on, and the deeper the water, the more it holds you back, walking or jumping. A breaking wave and a current push you while your feet are in the water.

## Swimming

Hold a direction to swim: each stroke pushes you through the water that way from where you look, with the power of a trained swimmer. You lie along your stroke, so the water drags you least that way. Look down to dive and you go down head first. You push hardest from a standstill, so you are under way within a second or two. In still water you swim about 1.2 m/s and glide on for a few seconds after you let go.

The water you swim in moves you too: swim with a wave's front face and it carries you faster than you swim. A river's current can be faster than you: then swim across it toward the bank, not against it, and let it carry you down.

## On the bottom

Where you sink to the bottom you stand on it on your feet, and walk on it as on land.

Press jump to push off the bottom with your legs. Alone it lifts you up toward the surface, a body laden past swimming up included. With a direction held you bound off the bottom that way, land and push off again, so even when you carry too much to swim up you can bound along the bottom to the shore.

## Getting out

Swim into the bank and you climb out of deep water, as in the base game. Where the water reaches only your chest you wade, and jump up a step as on land.

## What you carry

Armour and the tools and weapons in your hotbar weigh on you, in the water and in a boat. Your backpack and materials don't: a stack of stones in your hand stays the game's convention.

In the water a thing weighs what it weighs in the air less the water it pushes aside. A tool's metal or stone head pulls you down, and its wooden haft holds you up; a head of the same size weighs by its own metal, so copper is heavier than iron and flint far lighter. | An iron axe | 1.20 kg | 0.71 kg |
| A flint axe | 0.60 kg | 0.11 kg |
| A copper pickaxe | 3.36 kg | 2.11 kg |
| A copper shovel | 2.58 kg | 1.83 kg |
| A copper scythe | 1.25 kg | nothing: its ash snath floats it |
| An iron sword | 1.19 kg | 1.04 kg |
| An iron spear, a bow, a shield | | less than nothing: their wood holds you up |
| Full iron plate | 35 kg | 31 kg |
| Full iron chain | 7.5 kg | 6.5 kg |

| Wooden armour | 7.5 kg | −3.5 kg: it holds you up a little |

Your arms lift about 7 kg of it in fresh water and 9 kg in the sea. Carry more and you can't swim up: the game tells you how much you carry. Move tools into your backpack, or push off the bottom and bound to the shore. Full plate takes you down fast.

## Going deep

Once your chest is about 6 m down, the water squeezes the air in your lungs so far that you sink with nothing on you, and you keep sinking until you swim up. Above that depth you rise on your own in the sea, slowly; in fresh water you sink at any depth unless you swim. A jump in can take you that deep.

## Breaking crests

A crest can break over you, as one breaks on a vessel: as often, by the same [wind](Wind-and-Waves). Its water is full of air and holds you up less, so you go under, and its roller pushes you along with the wave. The air clears within a wave or so and you come back up. The next crest can break before you do.

In [surf](Surf-and-Swamping#breakers) the tallest waves break, and close to the beach nearly all of them.

Holding jump doesn't keep you up through a breaker: its frothy water holds you up less, and your stroke can't make up the difference. A small one takes you a hand's breadth under, a big one a metre or more. You come back up once its air clears.

The world setting [Breakers on swimmers](World-Settings#the-mechanics) turns breaking crests off. You still float on the waves and breathe by them.

## A swamped boat

When your boat [swamps](Surf-and-Swamping#swamping), you go into the water at its side and hold on to it. Bail it out, wait for a lull, or let go and swim.
