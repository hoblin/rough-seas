In the water your body floats by what it displaces, not by a rule of the game. It rises, lies and heaves by the weight of the water it pushes aside, under the waves you see.

## Floating

Left alone in the sea you float with the water at your face, and the waves carry you up, down and to and fro. In a calm sea you can just breathe. In a rough one the crests wash over your head and the troughs give your air back.

Fresh water holds you up less, so in a lake or a river you sink slowly unless you swim. Swimming is the game's own stroke, and holding jump treads water with your head clear.

## What you carry

Armour and the tools and weapons in your hotbar weigh on you, in the water and in a boat. Your backpack and materials don't: a stack of stones in your hand stays the game's convention. Move your tools into the backpack before a long swim, as you would leave a toolbox ashore.

You have about a kilogram and a half to spare in the sea. A knife only sinks you a little lower, an axe takes you under unless you tread water, and full plate takes you down fast. Extra weight doesn't drown you by itself: it makes you swim to stay up.

## Going deep

Below about 7 m the water squeezes the air in your lungs so far that you sink without any weight on you, and you keep sinking until you swim up. Above that depth you rise on your own, slowly.

## Breaking crests

While [dangerous oceans](World-Settings) are on, a crest can break over you, as one breaks on a vessel: as often, by the same [wind](Wind-and-Waves). Its water is full of air and holds you up less, so you go under, and its roller pushes you along with the wave. The air clears within a wave or so and you come back up. The next crest can break before you do.

In [surf](Surf-and-Swamping), in water shallower than the waves can stand, every wave breaks. That is where swimmers get into real trouble.

Holding jump is strong enough to swim straight out of a breaker for now. Stamina, planned for [The living sea](Roadmap#the-living-sea), will make that effort cost. The formulas are in [The science of the sea](The-Science-of-the-Sea#a-body-in-the-water).
