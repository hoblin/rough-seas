![The sailboat afloat](images/sailboat/afloat-bow.webp)

The game's own sailing boat: an open hull of planks on a keel, with a mast stepped amidships carrying one square sail on a yard, a steering oar aft and room for cargo. It is built in place on the shore, plank by plank, and launched from there.

## In Rough Seas

The vessel for long voyages and heavy cargo. Its hull takes knocks that would break anything else, its 1500-litre bilge gives you time in a sea, and it is the one vessel that [anchors](Anchoring). Its mast draws [lightning](Lightning), its sides catch the wind that strains a [mooring](Mooring), and its 300 planks make it the dearest thing afloat to lose. In a gale it ships water faster than one person can [bail](Bailing): run for shelter, or anchor well out and ride it out. Two fifths of its weight is ballast, so flooded it goes to the bottom with its cargo, unless it is built of a light wood: of pine, larch, acacia, walnut, bald cypress, redwood or kapok it floats awash.

## Where it comes from

Open, square-sailed boats of planks carried the coastal trade of northern Europe for over a thousand years, from the Norse boats of the Viking age to the working boats of every fishing harbour. One square sail on a yard drives them well with the wind behind and poorly against it, and a single oar over the quarter steers them. The game's sailboat is a boat of that family, small enough for one or two to sail.

Its sail drives it the same whatever the wind does; sailing by the wind comes in [Seamanship](Roadmap#seamanship).

## Its neighbours

- The [outrigger canoe](Outrigger-Canoe) is steadier and carries one basket; she sails slower for now.
- The [hide currach](Hide-Currach) is the small, cheap boat for the coast, and it rides over surf that would swamp the sailboat, though a breaker that stoves in her hide leaves the sailboat's planks whole.
