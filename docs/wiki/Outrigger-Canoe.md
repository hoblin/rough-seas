A dugout with a float log held out to one side on three booms lashed across her gunwales, and a mast stayed fore, aft and out to the float. Her crab-claw sail hangs between two spars, set, reefed or furled to the upper one. Her hull, her float and her poles can each be of their own wood.

## In Rough Seas

For a long crossing of open sea. The outrigger is the fastest vessel before metal, and it sails, leaving every rowed boat far astern. The float keeps the narrow hull upright where a dugout would roll over, though her low sides ship water as a dugout's do. It is clumsy to turn and it can't be picked up, so where you launch it is where it lives.

## Where it comes from

The outrigger is the boat of the Austronesian expansion, the greatest migration by sea before modern times: from Taiwan and Island Southeast Asia, canoes like it carried people west to Madagascar and east across the Pacific to Hawaii, Rapa Nui and New Zealand. A burnt and hewn dugout hull carries a single float (the *ama*), a smaller log shaped with an adze and pointed at its ends, on booms lashed across the hull with fibre cord, and a crab-claw sail of woven matting on two spars. All of it was made without metal or saw. The model is a small single-outrigger sailing canoe of that family, with linen in place of the matting.

## How it is built

Not in the crafting grid: an outrigger is a finished [dugout](Dugout) rigged where she lies, piece by piece, each piece drawn on her as it goes on. Nothing in it needs a saw.

1. **Split the poles.** Lay three logs of one wood on their sides, end to end, anywhere, set a [splitting wedge](Recipes#splitting-wedge) on each, and hold an axe on a wedge: the trunk splits into 6 poles, every one she takes. Any wood will do, a modded tree's too.

   ![Three logs with a wedge on each](images/outrigger-canoe/build-poles.webp)

2. **Lay her float.** Beside a finished dugout, lay three more logs of one wood on their sides, end to end along her, a block off: hull, gap, float.

   ![A dugout with three logs alongside](images/outrigger-canoe/build-float.webp)

3. **Join them.** Hold a pole on the dugout: her first boom goes across her onto the logs, and they become her float. She is now the outrigger, her hull the same hull, with its cracks and the water in it. A float on the far side turns her end for end.

   ![The first boom across](images/outrigger-canoe/build-boom.webp)

4. **Boom and lash her.** Hold the other two poles on her for the fore and aft booms, then rope to lash each boom to both gunwales and to the float: thirteen turns a lashing, 56 metres in all, six ropes. All her poles must be of one wood.

   ![Three booms lashed](images/outrigger-canoe/build-lashed.webp)

5. **Hew the float.** Hold an axe on her to hew each end of the float to a point.
6. **Step the mast.** Hold the last three poles on her: the mast, then the crab claw's upper and lower spars.

   ![The float hewn, the mast and spars up](images/outrigger-canoe/build-mast.webp)

7. **Bend on the sail and set up the stays.** Hold linen cloth on her, four pieces for her 3.5 m² of sail, then four ropes: the forestay, the backstay, the shroud out to the float and the sheet. She is finished and takes her seats.

   ![Rigged, the sail furled on its spar](images/outrigger-canoe/build-rigged.webp)

Push her into the water with Shift and an empty hand: she is heavy, a few centimetres a push over dry ground, so rig her at the water's edge. An outrigger from before the build loads finished.

## Its neighbours

- The [hide currach](Hide-Currach) is the other small boat for the sea: slower, but it carries cargo and can be carried.
- The [sailboat](Sailboat) is slower and carries far more.
- The [dugout](Dugout) is the same hull without the float: she is built on one, and is as tough, and tippy at sea.
