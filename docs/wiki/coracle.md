# Coracle

![The coracle afloat](images/coracle/afloat-bow.webp)

A round bowl of hide stretched over a woven frame of sticks, sealed with rendered fat, with a single seat across the middle. It carries one person and a paddle, and it is light enough to wear on your back.

## In Rough Seas

For fishing and short crossings. The coracle is the only vessel that rides in the backpack slot, so it goes wherever you walk and is in the water again the moment you reach it. It turns on the spot and makes poor headway in a straight line. Its low rim swamps in anything more than a chop: keep it to rivers, lakes and sheltered bays.

## Where it comes from

Coracles have been paddled on the rivers of Wales, Ireland and Scotland since before the Romans arrived, and round hide or basket boats of the same kind were built on the Tigris (the *quffa*), in southern India and in Vietnam. A Welsh coracle is a lattice of split willow laths with a hide or, later, tarred calico over it, and a coracle-man walked home from the river with the boat on his back, held by a strap across the seat. The model is that boat: laths on the floor and up the sides, a woven rim, one seat.
