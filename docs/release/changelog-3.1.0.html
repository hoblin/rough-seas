3.1 makes every blow harm a hull the way it would a real one. What hits it, and what it hits, decides now, not a roll of the dice.

What harms a boat now:

  • Other boats. Run into another vessel and both hulls take the knock, shared by their masses. A light boat rammed by a heavy one suffers; the heavy one hardly notices.
  • Clubs, spears and fists break only the plank they land on. A sailboat's thin planks give where a dugout's thick walls hold.
  • Axes and saws cut. A planked hull breaks up once every place is stove in, so an axe takes a boat apart on the beach too.
  • Breakers slam the windward side, and either the planks hold or they're stove in. A spilling whitecap hits gently. A plunging crest slams about nine times harder, and on a steep beach most waves plunge. A storm's sea stoves in the coracle and the currach.
  • Thrown ashore, a moored vessel hits the bottom at the speed of the breakers that threw it.

What doesn't:

  • Creatures and swimmers. They only push a boat.
  • A breaker on the raft or the sailboat. Their logs and planks stand any sea.
  • A club or a breaker on the reed boat: the bundles spring back. A blade still parts its lashings, and a hard knock on rock tears it, shown now as Reed torn.
  • A hide hull springs back from a blow, unless a lath backs it right there.
  • Bad luck. Each blow meets a piece of wood as strong as real timber happens to be, no stronger and no weaker.

The handbook and the wiki say what each vessel stands: Hull condition, Breakers.

For modders: a vessel gets a shell, what a blow, a cut and a breaker meet. No documented key was removed, but a vessel of yours fares differently: two hulls ram, a reed hull tears, and one without a shell takes no harm from breakers. These config/storms.json keys are gone:

  • waves.breakerTransfer: a breaker's slam pressure, set against the planking's bending strength.
  • waves.periodSecondsPerRootMetre: a breaker's speed follows from its height at the steepest a wave can stand, capped by the sea's own period.
  • ashore.impactFactor: a thrown vessel strikes as running aground at the breaker's speed.
