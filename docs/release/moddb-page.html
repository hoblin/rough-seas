In vanilla the sea is flat, safe and empty. Rough Seas gives it a wind you sail by, storms, waves and currents, hulls that crack and flood, a body that floats by the water it pushes aside, and six new vessels between the raft and the sailboat. Every mechanic is worked out from physics and measured data.

What changes at sea

Wind. The real world's prevailing winds blow over the sea. They push everything afloat, and a sail goes by them.

More about the wind
  • The wind has a direction and a strength. Its direction follows the Earth's prevailing winds by latitude: the trade winds, the westerlies, the polar easterlies.
  • It changes slowly. A spell of wind lasts most of a day, so the wind you leave with is usually the wind for the whole crossing.
  • You can read it by looking around: trees sway with its strength, the clouds travel with it, and the coordinates readout names where it blows from and how hard.
  • It pushes every vessel, paddled and rowed too: sideways as you go, downwind when you leave it alone, and a light one with high sides the most.
  • A sail goes by the wind: well with it abeam or behind, slowly close to it, and nowhere straight into it. On an island with the wind against you, you may wait for it to turn.

More: Wind, sailing the outrigger and the sailboat.

Weather. Storms are born where and when real ones are, cross the world on a path, and warn you before they arrive.

More about the weather
  • A storm is a real thing in the world, with a place, a size and a path. The same storm reaches a ship at sea and the coast it is heading for, hours apart.
  • There are squalls, gales and hurricanes. They travel with the prevailing wind, so the sky upwind is where weather comes from.
  • A storm warns you first: the treetops sway harder, the clouds thicken and darken, rain begins. The sea rises after the wind, since waves take hours to build, and runs high for hours after it drops.
  • All rain and every cloud come from the same weather. Where it is fair, no rain falls.
  • Warm storms bring lightning, and a mast draws it.

More: Storms, Lightning.

Sea. Waves grow from the wind, you see them as high as they are, and they break on the shore.

More about the sea
  • Waves grow from three things: how hard the wind blows, how much open water it crosses, and how long it has blown. A pond barely ripples in a gale, and a big lake raises a sea of its own.
  • Every wave is different. Most are lower than the sea's height, and a rare one is twice as high.
  • You see the sea as it is: a swell as high as the waves that strike your hull, and whitecaps by the wind's force.
  • Waves break on the shore and over bars, run up the beach and slide back, and stand up or throw spray against a cliff or a wall.
  • The oceans turn in slow gyres, and their currents carry everything afloat, a boat or a swimmer.
  • You hear it too: each passing wave splashes at your hull, and a breaker slams into it.

More: Waves, The look of the sea, Currents, Sound credits.

Boats. A vessel floats by its mass, ships water over its side, cracks where a knock lands, leaks and floods.

More about the boats
  • Every vessel floats by its mass: its wood, its load and the water in it. Loaded, it sits lower and runs slower.
  • Waves come over a low side and rain falls in. You bail with a bowl, a jug or a bucket.
  • A knock cracks the hull where it lands, and the crack leaks. Look at a vessel to read each cracked place, how fast it leaks and how much side it still shows above the water.
  • A hull is lost by flooding far more often than by breaking. Flooded, a light hull lies awash and a heavy one sinks.
  • You mend a hull with its own material, on the place you look at.
  • The dugout, the outrigger, the bark canoe and the currach carry cargo loose on their floors, lying there as small piles.
  • White water batters every hull but the bark canoe's and the coracle's.
  • A boat left afloat is carried off by the wind and the current. Any vessel ties to a post, and the sailboat anchors. A rope or an anchor holds until the pull is more than it bears.

More: Hull condition, Surf and swamping, Bailing, Mending, Holds, Rapids, Mooring, Anchoring.

Swimming. You float by your own body and swim with a trained swimmer's strength, and what you carry weighs on you by what it is made of.

More about swimming
  • You float by the water your body pushes aside: with your face clear in the sea, and lower in fresh water.
  • You swim with the strength of a trained swimmer, pushing hardest from a standstill.
  • Your armour and the tools in your hotbar weigh on you by what they are made of. Metal pulls you down, and wood holds you up.
  • Carry too much and you can't swim up: the game tells you so. You stand on the bottom, walk along it to the shore, or push off it with your legs.
  • The water moves you. Waves lift you, a current carries you, and a breaking crest pushes you under until its air clears.
  • Jump in and you keep your way into the water.

More: Swimming.

The vessels

Vanilla gives you the raft and the sailboat and nothing in between. Rough Seas adds six, each from a real boatbuilding tradition, each with its own job, none best at everything. You build each one in place, step by step: its wiki page shows how.

Raft · 0.45 m/s
Floating cargo downriver. A few hard knocks shake its lashings loose.

Reed boat (new) · 2.1 m/s
A day afloat, bound from two reed bundles. Sinks within four days unless dried; can't be mended.

Dugout (new) · 1.7 m/s
The tough workhorse of rivers and lakes. Heavy, and tippy at sea.

Coracle (new) · 1 m/s
Fishing and short hops, worn on your back. Slow, swamps in a chop.

Bark canoe (new) · 2.1 m/s
Long river trips, through rapids. Fragile.

Hide currach (new) · 2.4 m/s, rowed
Your first trip along the coast, cargo included. Needs greasing; one hard knock holes it.

Outrigger canoe (new) · sails by the wind
Crossings of open sea, close to the wind. Stays where launched.

Sailboat · sails by the wind
Long voyages and heavy cargo; the one that anchors. Expensive to build, ships water fast in a gale.

Speeds are one rower's, paddled unless noted, on calm water; a sail goes as fast as the wind lets it. The dugout and the outrigger build from any tree, other mods' trees too, and the harder the wood, the tougher the hull.

What's new in 5.0

  • Sailing by the wind. The outrigger and the sailboat sail by where the wind blows from: well with it abeam or behind, slowly close to it, and nowhere straight into it. The wind pushes them sideways as they sail and drifts them with the sail down, and a sail set at a mooring strains the rope. More: Under sail.
  • The wind on every vessel. The wind pushes the paddled and rowed vessels too, sideways as you go and downwind when left alone, harder the stronger it blows and the more they show above water. More: Wind.
  • The outrigger rigged as history built it. Its mast stands by the float's side, with a shroud and a shore out to the float and running stays to both ends; the yard hangs on a halyard. It keeps its float to windward and changes tack by shunting. It sails whatever you hold, where before it needed a paddle in hand. More: Sailing the outrigger.
  • Sails that fill with the wind. Both sails are drawn as cloth that fills, trims itself and heaves with the hull. More: Sailing the sailboat.
  • Swimming, reworked. You swim with a trained swimmer's strength. What you carry weighs by its parts: a tool's metal head pulls you down, and its wooden haft holds you up. On the bottom you stand, walk and push off with your legs, and the game tells you when you carry too much to swim up. A jump into the water keeps its way. More: Swimming.
  • Easier, if you want it. Three new world settings: two make the wind in your sails and your swimming stroke two or three times as strong, and one gives you the base game's swimming back. More: World settings.

The whole list: Roadmap.

Install

  • Vintage Story 1.22.4 or later.
  • Download the zip from this page and drop it into your Mods folder (main menu, Mods → Open Mods Folder). Don't unzip it.
  • Multiplayer: supported. The server and every client need the mod.

Early days

Rough Seas is very young. Like any software, it takes time and effort to polish everything, so expect rough edges, and make a backup before adding it to your existing save: the mod is designed to be safe to turn on and off, but still.

Your reports really help. When something goes wrong, tell us in the comments here or in GitHub issues, and attach your game's log if you can (the Logs folder next to your Mods folder): the mod writes down every decision it makes, so the log shows us what happened.

World settings

  • Rough Seas playstyle on the new world screen: standard survival with far more open ocean.
  • A setting for each mechanic, all on by default. Switch off the ones you don't want, and make sailing and swimming easier if you like: World settings.
  • An existing world gets every mechanic and every vessel. It just has less sea to cross.
Tested compatibility

Tested on Vintage Story 1.22.7:

  • Dynamic Seasonal Temperature 1.4.0: compatible.
  • Better and Improved Weather Continued 2.0.5: compatible.
  • Realistic Water 0.4.0: compatible.
  • Realistic Rapids 0.1.3: compatible.
  • Extreme Weathers 1.8.5: compatible. Its own hurricane, monsoon and blizzard rolls are off, since our storms bring them. Its damage, howl, tornadoes and deep snow ride our storms. No whiteout for now.
  • Terra Prety 7.11.2-pre.1: compatible. Set world height 448 when you create the world, or its seas are too shallow for storms to raise much of a sea.
  • Wildcraft: Trees and Shrubs 1.3.4: compatible, and its woods build our vessels.
  • SheyderMod 2.1.1: compatible from Rough Seas 4.4.1.
  • Shipworks+ 1.1.0: works with issues. Its ships sail in our wind and feel our sea, but don't yet rise and roll with our drawn swell.
  • Shipwright 1.5.1: compatible. Its boats are vessels of ours.
  • Walkable Entities 1.0.44: compatible. A deck rides our swell with whoever stands on it. Our own vessels can't be walked on.

What each pair does together: Beside other mods. Running Rough Seas with a mod not listed here? Tell us how it went.

Switched off for now
  • Capsizing. Breakers still ship water and slam the hull, but nothing overturns. It comes back with heel in "Seamanship".
  • The sea's own sound. The open sea, the surf and the whitecaps' hiss are off until they are redone from real recordings. The shore laps with the game's own sound, and every wave at a hull, every breaker and every knock still sounds.
  • Swimming tires you no more than in vanilla. Enjoy it while it lasts ;)
