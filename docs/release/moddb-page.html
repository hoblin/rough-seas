In vanilla the sea is flat, safe and empty. Rough Seas gives it weather, storms and currents, hulls that crack and flood, and six new vessels between the raft and the sailboat.

Paddle, pole, row

Every vessel moves by its own physics: what you push with, how hard you can push, and how the hull slips through the water.

Paddle along the side, and change sides to keep your course.

Pole off the bottom through the shallows, as long as the pole reaches.

Row the currach on its thole pins.

Scull the coracle over its bow.

The vessels

Vanilla gives you the raft and the sailboat and nothing in between. Rough Seas adds six, each from a real boatbuilding tradition, each with its own job, none best at everything. You build each one in place, step by step: its wiki page shows how.

Raft · 0.45 m/s
Floating cargo downriver. A few hard knocks shake its lashings loose.

Reed boat (new) · 2.1 m/s
A day afloat, bound from two reed bundles. Sinks within four days unless dried; can't be mended.

Dugout (new) · 1.7 m/s
The tough workhorse of rivers and lakes. Heavy, and tippy at sea.

Coracle (new) · 1 m/s
Fishing and short hops, worn on your back. Slow, swamps in a chop.

Bark canoe (new) · 2.1 m/s
Long river trips, through rapids. Fragile.

Hide currach (new) · 2.4 m/s, rowed
Your first trip along the coast, cargo included. Needs greasing; one hard knock holes it.

Outrigger canoe (new) · 5.9 m/s, sailing
Fast crossings of open sea. Carries little, stays where launched.

Sailboat · 7.2 m/s, sailing
Long voyages and heavy cargo; the one that anchors. Expensive to build, ships water fast in a gale.

Speeds are one rower's, paddled unless noted, on calm water; under full sail vanilla's pace, whatever the wind. The dugout and the outrigger build from any tree, other mods' trees too, and the harder the wood, the tougher the hull.

Everything else at sea

  • Wind and waves. The real world's prevailing winds; waves grow from the wind's strength, the open water and the hours it has blown, so a big lake raises a sea of its own. More: Wind and waves.
  • Storms. Squalls, gales and hurricanes cross the world, born where and when real ones are. Each warns you first. Warm storms strike, and a mast draws the lightning. More: Storms, Lightning.
  • Currents. The oceans turn in slow gyres and carry every boat, paddled or left alone. More: Currents.
  • Cracks and leaks. A knock cracks the hull where it lands. Look at a vessel to read each cracked place, the leak in litres a minute and its freeboard. More: Hull condition.
  • Mending. Hold the hull's own material and right-click the place to patch it. More: Mending.
  • Surf. Waves break on the shore and over bars, run up the beach and slide back, and stand up or throw spray against a cliff or a wall. More: The look of the sea.
  • Swimming. You float by your body, weighed down by armour and tools, and a breaking crest drives you under. More: Swimming.
  • Floating and flooding. Every vessel floats by its mass: wood, load and the water in it. Flooded, a light hull lies awash and a heavy one sinks. More: Surf and swamping.
  • Bailing. A bowl, jug or bucket, one held click. More: Bailing.
  • Rapids. White water batters every hull but the bark canoe's and the coracle's. More: Rapids.
  • Anchoring and mooring. Any vessel ties to a post at the length you set, and the sailboat's stone anchor holds by its weight on the bottom. A hard jerk parts the rope, and a storm drags the anchor. More: Anchoring, Mooring.
  • A sea you can hear. Each passing wave splashes at your hull, a breaker slams into it, and the hull creaks and cracks as it takes knocks. The open sea's sound, the surf and the whitecaps are switched off for now, until they are redone from real recordings; the shore laps with the game's own sound. Sound credits.

What's new in 4.0

  • Vessels built in place. Burn out and hew a dugout from a log, with a real fire, hewing you can see and clay. Strip a bark canoe off a standing tree and frame it where it falls. Bind a reed boat from two bundles of dried reeds. Lay laths for a coracle or a currach and cover them with hides that rot unless prepared. Rig an outrigger on a finished dugout. More: Building a vessel.
  • Poles, laths and wedges. Split a row of logs into poles with wedges, blow by blow, and the poles into laths; soak the laths so they bend. More: Recipes.
  • Paddle, pole and oars, by the rower. Speed comes from the rower's strength and the hull. Paddle on both sides, changing sides as the boat veers. Pole standing, where the hull bears you, as far as the pole reaches the bottom. Row the currach facing its stern, on its thole pins. Paddle the raft kneeling. More: Paddles, poles and oars.
  • Hulls you can see. Cracks show on the hull where they are. A blow on a framed hull lands on its skin and the frame behind. The outrigger's float rides on lashings of its own. More: Hull condition.
  • Afloat by mass. A vessel heaves by its own mass, and a rider's weight comes aboard as they sit down. More: The science of the hull.
  • Launching on foot. Push a heavy vessel into the water yourself. More: Building a vessel.
  • Under a crest. Your eye goes under a crest that passes over it, and you hold your breath by the sea you see, on a server too. Both are the work of our first contributor, rhino1998. More: Reading the sea.
  • The handbook rewritten, and every one of its 33 languages corrected.

The whole list: Roadmap.

Install

  • Vintage Story 1.22.0 or later.
  • Download the zip from this page and drop it into your Mods folder (main menu, Mods → Open Mods Folder). Don't unzip it.
  • Multiplayer: supported. The server and every client need the mod.

Early days

Rough Seas is very young. Like any software, it takes time and effort to polish everything, so expect rough edges, and make a backup before adding it to your existing save: the mod is designed to be safe to turn on and off, but still.

Your reports really help. When something goes wrong, tell us in the comments here or in GitHub issues, and attach your game's log if you can (the Logs folder next to your Mods folder): the mod writes down every decision it makes, so the log shows us what happened.

World settings

  • Rough Seas playstyle on the new world screen: standard survival with far more open ocean.
  • A setting for each mechanic, all on by default: World settings.
  • An existing world gets every mechanic and every vessel. It just has less sea to cross.
Tested compatibility

Tested on Vintage Story 1.22.7:

  • Dynamic Seasonal Temperature 1.4.0: compatible.
  • Better and Improved Weather Continued 2.0.5: compatible.
  • Realistic Water 0.4.0: compatible.
  • Realistic Rapids 0.1.3: compatible.
  • Extreme Weathers 1.8.5: compatible. Its own hurricane, monsoon and blizzard rolls are off, since our storms bring them. Its damage, howl, tornadoes and deep snow ride our storms. No whiteout for now.
  • Terra Prety 7.11.2-pre.1: compatible. Set world height 448 when you create the world, or its seas are too shallow for storms to raise much of a sea.
  • Wildcraft: Trees and Shrubs 1.3.4: compatible, and its woods build our vessels.
  • SheyderMod 2.1.1: compatible from Rough Seas 4.4.1.
  • Shipworks+ 1.1.0: works with issues. Its ships sail in our wind and feel our sea, but don't yet rise and roll with our drawn swell.

What each pair does together: Beside other mods. Running Rough Seas with a mod not listed here? Tell us how it went.

Switched off for now
  • Capsizing. Breakers still ship water and slam the hull, but nothing overturns. It comes back with heel in "Seamanship", as part of sailing by the wind.
  • Sails ignore the wind, and swimming tires you no more than in vanilla. Enjoy it while it lasts ;)

Learning it

The handbook explains it from a player's point of view: open the Guides tab for Going to Sea. The wiki goes deeper, with the numbers and the real-world data behind them. The mod speaks all 33 languages the game supports. If you're a native speaker and something reads wrong, corrections are very welcome.
