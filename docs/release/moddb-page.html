In vanilla the sea is flat, safe and empty. Crossing it means holding W on a raft. Rough Seas gives the sea weather of its own: wind, storms that cross the world, waves that grow with them, currents. Every vessel wears, swamps and breaks by what it's made of, and six new vessels fill the gap between the raft and the sailboat.

It's an early stage of development, so make a backup before adding it to your existing save. The mod is designed to be safe to turn on and off, but still.

Tested compatibility

Tested on Vintage Story 1.22.7:

  • Dynamic Seasonal Temperature 1.4.0: compatible.
  • Better and Improved Weather Continued 2.0.5: compatible.
  • Realistic Water 0.4.0: compatible.
  • Realistic Rapids 0.1.3: compatible.
  • Extreme Weathers 1.8.5: compatible. Its own hurricane, monsoon and blizzard rolls are off, since our storms bring them. Its damage, howl, tornadoes and deep snow ride our storms. No whiteout for now.
  • Terra Prety 7.11.2-pre.1: compatible. Set world height 448 when you create the world, or its seas are too shallow for storms to raise much of a sea.
  • Wildcraft: Trees and Shrubs 1.3.4: compatible, and its woods build our vessels.
  • SheyderMod 2.0.0: compatible from Rough Seas 2.0.1.
  • Shipworks+ 1.1.0: works with issues. It made itself compatible with us; its ships sail in our wind and feel our sea, but don't yet rise and roll with our drawn swell.

Thanks

Rough Seas started with Mahjong Blonsky's video Why doesn't Vintage Story have oceans?. We liked his ideas so much that we built them. Thanks for the great ideas, and for asking the question.

The sea

  • Wind and waves. Wind follows the real world's prevailing winds, and waves grow from its strength, the open water and the hours it has blown, so a big lake raises a sea of its own.
  • Storms. Squalls, gales and hurricanes cross the world, born where and when real ones are. Each warns you first.
  • A sea you can read. The swell is drawn as high as the waves that hit your hull, and whitecaps spread by the Beaufort scale.
  • Lightning. Warm storms strike, and a mast draws it.
  • Currents. The oceans turn in slow gyres and carry off a boat left alone.

Your vessel

  • Hull condition. Every knock is weighed against what the hull can take, and the hull shows it: scratches first, then splits, tears and holes.
  • Mending. Hold the hull's own material and right-click. Hard work, and it makes you hungry.
  • Surf and swamping. Waves come over a low side and break in the shallows. A full bilge swamps the boat; only the ballasted sailboat sinks.
  • Bailing. A bowl, jug or bucket, one held click.
  • Rapids. White water batters every hull but the bark canoe's.
  • Anchoring and mooring. The sailboat's anchor holds as well as its rope allows, and any vessel ties to a post. Ropes snap.

The vessels

Vanilla gives you the raft and the sailboat and nothing in between. Rough Seas adds six. Each comes from a real boatbuilding tradition and has its own job. None is best at everything. Pretty much how armor works in vanilla.

From any wood. The dugout and the outrigger build from any tree, other mods' trees too, and the harder the wood, the tougher the hull. The bark canoe builds from birch, elm or spruce bark.

What's new in 2.0

  • Under vanilla. Rough Seas' weather now drives the game's own wind, rain and weather events, so other weather mods build on it instead of fighting it.
  • Tested beside other mods, listed above.
  • Waves from the water. Fetch, depth and the hours the wind has blown, as coastal engineers predict them. Lakes get waves now.
  • Storms on real climatology. Gales in the storm-track belts, hurricanes over warm tropical seas in their season.
  • Vessels from any wood, other mods' trees too.
  • The wind on the coordinates readout, in every language the game speaks.

The whole list: Roadmap.

World settings

  • Rough Seas playstyle on the new world screen: standard survival with far more open ocean.
  • Dangerous oceans, on by default. Turn it off and the sea keeps its weather and currents, but storms, surf and swamping do no harm: /worldconfig dangerousOceans false.

An existing world gets every mechanic and every vessel. It just has less sea to cross. More: World settings.

Switched off in 2.0

  • Capsizing. Breakers still ship water and strike the hull, but nothing overturns. It comes back with heel in "Seamanship", as part of sailing by the wind.
  • Surf foam on the beach. It didn't read as surf yet. It comes back once it does. Whitecaps out at sea stay.

And still not there: sails ignore the wind, and nothing harms a swimmer. Enjoy it while it lasts ;)

Learning it

The handbook explains it from a player's point of view: open the Guides tab for Going to Sea. The wiki goes deeper, with the numbers and the real-world data behind them. The mod speaks all 33 languages the game supports. If you're a native speaker and something reads wrong, corrections are very welcome.

Install

  1. Vintage Story 1.22.7.
  2. Download the zip from this page.
  3. Drop it into your Mods folder (main menu, Mods → Open Mods Folder). Don't unzip it.

Multiplayer: supported. The server and every client need the mod.

Coming next

Plans, not promises (roadmap):

  • "Seamanship": sailing by the wind, leeway, an anchored boat swinging bow to the wind, rowing on one side to turn, a windsock, masts that break, and heel with capsizing back.
  • "The living sea": swimming that wears you out, cold water that kills at real-world rates, sharks, whales and rust creatures.
  • "The deep": a Jonas-tech diving suit, harpoons, depth sounding, shipwrecks, raising your own sunk vessel, wandering traders, metal boats.

Problems and ideas

Found a bug or have an idea? The issue tracker or the comments here both work. For a crash, please attach client-main.log, or server-main.log on a server. They're in the game's data folder under Logs.
