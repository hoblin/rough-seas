4.3: the sea moves your boat, and the rope holds it by physics.

  • The swell carries every hull. A boat sways to and fro with the waves by its own size, heading and draft, whether you sit in it or not; a big hull sways less than a small one. More: Reading the sea.
  • Currents carry every vessel. A current carries your boat while you paddle or row it too, not only when it lies empty, and a heavy boat takes longer to come up to the current's speed. A moored boat swings to lie along the stream. More: Currents.
  • A mooring rope that holds. A rope tied to a post holds the boat at the length you set in the rope's tool modes (F). It parts only when the boat comes to its end hard or the sea pulls past what the rope bears, and a parted rope falls by its post. A boat takes one rope. The endless untie-and-retie loop of an old world is fixed. More: Mooring.
  • An anchor that holds by its weight. The sailboat's 100 kg stone holds by its grip on the bottom, better on rock than on sand. A strong current or a storm drags it, and the stone still slows the boat as it slides. Tie up and drop anchor at once, and both hold. More: Anchoring.
  • Each world setting says what it turns off and what stays, in every language.
